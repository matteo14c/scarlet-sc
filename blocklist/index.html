    The blocklist¶

    Before it searches for markers, SCARLET keeps some genes out of them: genes that describe what happened to a cell or what state it is in, rather than what type it is. A ribosomal or a dissociation-stress gene can be detected more often in one cell type of a reference only because of how that sample was handled; as a marker it would then call cells by their handling instead of their type. The blocklist is applied by learn and transfer; it removes genes from the marker search only (the counts are not changed, and assign scores the model's markers as they are).

    Where it is set: --blocklist in learn and transfer; --blocklist-modules in Advanced options. Its messages are explained in Troubleshooting.

    What is on it¶

    The bundled lists (human and mouse) are divided into six gene groups (modules):

    group human genes applied what it holds how the genes were chosen
    core_stress 21 always immediate-early and heat-shock genes (FOS, FOSB, JUN, JUNB, JUND, EGR1, ATF3, DUSP1, IER2, IER3, ZFP36, PPP1R15A, HSPA1A, HSPA1B, HSPA6, DNAJB1, DNAJA1, HSP90AA1, HSPH1, HSPB1, BAG3) induced by tissue handling in at least two of three published dissociation signatures (van den Brink et al. 2017; O'Flanagan et al. 2019; Denisenko et al. 2020), or strictly heat-inducible
    core_ribosomal 265 always the 79 ribosome-subunit genes and 186 ribosomal-protein pseudogenes HGNC gene families and locus types; paralogs with their own functions (e.g. RPL3L, RPS4Y2), the S6 kinases and read-through transcripts are not on it
    core_mitochondrial 49 always the mitochondrially encoded genes and MTRNR2L1–12 the mitochondrial chromosome (HGNC)
    core_sex 8 always XIST, TSIX and six Y-linked genes (DDX3Y, EIF1AY, KDM5D, USP9Y, UTY, RPS4Y1) HGNC
    cell_cycle 97 unless a label names dividing cells the S and G2/M phase genes of Tirosh et al. 2016, with current gene symbols as distributed with Seurat (cc.genes)
    stress_extended 14 never by default activation and quiescence genes with weaker evidence of handling stress (EGR2, EGR3, NR4A1, NFKBIA, SOCS3, DUSP2, BTG1, BTG2, ZFP36L1, ZFP36L2, IER5, DNAJA4, DNAJB4, RHOB) in only one or two of the studies above, or markers of real cell states

    The mouse list mirrors the human groups gene for gene through orthology (MGI homology classes, with Ensembl as a cross-check), plus the mouse ribosomal-protein pseudogenes, which have no human counterparts, and the withdrawn symbols MGI lists for them so that older gene names still match. Its groups hold 51 stress, 1,195 ribosomal, 100 mitochondrial, 16 sex-linked and 292 cell-cycle rows, and 38 rows of stress_extended, counting the aliases.

    Genes are matched by Ensembl id or by symbol (case-insensitive), whichever the data have.

    How SCARLET chooses¶

    The species (blocklist, --blocklist; default auto): from the gene names. Mostly upper-case symbols (CD3E) or ENSG ids mean human; mostly capitalised symbols (Cd3e) or ENSMUSG ids mean mouse. When the names do not tell, SCARLET stops with an error and asks you to choose.

    value what it does
    auto (default) the bundled human or mouse list, chosen from the gene names
    human, mouse the bundled list for that species
    none no gene is kept out
    a file path your own tab-separated list, with a Symbol and/or an ENSG_ID column (with a Category column holding the group names above, blocklist_modules acts on it too); a path that does not exist is an error

    The groups (blocklist_modules, --blocklist-modules; default auto): the four core_ groups always apply and stress_extended never does. The cell_cycle group applies unless one of the reference's labels names a dividing or proliferative state or stage (for example "cycling", "proliferating", "mitotic", "progenitor", "erythroblast"): in a reference that labels such cells, the cell-cycle genes are what tells them apart, so they must stay available as markers. The rule reads the labels you learn on, never a result. The pre-flight, the run's summary and the report say which list and which groups were used, and, when the cell-cycle genes stayed in, which labels kept them.

    Changing it¶

    • Another set of groups: blocklist_modules="all" applies every group (stress_extended included); a comma-separated list applies exactly those groups, e.g. --blocklist-modules core_stress,core_ribosomal.
    • No blocklist: blocklist="none" (--blocklist none).
    • Your own list: blocklist="my_list.tsv", a tab-separated file with a Symbol and/or ENSG_ID column. To add genes to the bundled list, copy it from the package (scarlet/scarlet_blocklist_universal.tsv for human, scarlet/scarlet_blocklist_mouse.tsv for mouse), add your rows and pass the file.

    Any of these is recorded in the model and the summary, and the report notes that the run is not at the package defaults.

    In the benchmarks¶

    The benchmarks ran every tool, SCARLET included, without a blocklist (blocklist none), so that all tools saw the same genes: none of the other tools removes genes. In normal use keep it on. See Benchmarks.

    Reading. On these data the blocklist makes little difference: every median F1 moves by 0.3 points or less, every share unlabelled by 0.3 points or less, and every share refused in Fig. 6 by 0.9 points or less, in both directions. These differences are of the size that the order of tied marker genes alone produces (up to about 1 point), so the benchmark neither shows a gain nor a cost from the blocklist. Its purpose is elsewhere: it keeps markers that describe how a sample was handled (stress, dissociation) or a cell's state out of a reference, which matters most when the reference and the query were handled differently, which the benchmark was not designed to test.
